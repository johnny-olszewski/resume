# Omada Health — APM, Data Governance

## Application Questions

---

### Q1: Provide an example of a tool or system you have developed or implemented to enable data analysis for technical and non-technical users. What challenges did you face, and how did you address them?

In my current role, I led the development of an experiment dashboard giving product managers, leadership, and other stakeholders a central location to view metadata, metrics, and results for current and historical A/B experiments. This has helped increase visibility to highlight experimentation as a tool we use to drive the best patient outcomes. The biggest challenge was aligning with product analytics and our broader data organization on metric definitions and validating the underlying pipelines for cross-team use. We addressed it by formalizing a definition and validation process as an SOP, ensuring every metric had documented lineage and cross-team sign-off before being surfaced in the dashboard.

---

### Q2: Describe a time you established or significantly improved data definitions or governance. What was broken, what you put in place, and what measurable impact did it have?

Previously, product managers independently requested data from product analytics to evaluate their experiments, which led to varying metric definitions and inconsistent results across teams. I addressed this by leading the development of a centralized experiment dashboard with standardized metric definitions agreed upon across product and product analytics. This consolidated what had been a fragmented, duplicative process, eliminating redundant analytical work on both sides and establishing a single source of truth that all teams could act on with confidence.
